# Historical Corpus Report

## Scope

This pass traces selected primary and vendor sources across the early-synth, software-EQ, and current periods. It tests for long-lived semantics and visual lineage separately. It does not claim a continuous or standardized glyph history.

## Findings

[ARP's 1971 2600 manual](https://archive.org/stream/synthmanual-arp-2600-owners-manual/arp2600ownersmanual_djvu.txt) records low-pass VCF material with explanatory, axis-bearing response treatment (EV-014). [Roland's JUPITER-8 retrospective](https://www.roland.com/us/products/rc_jupiter-8/) describes the 1981 instrument's low-pass and high-pass filter pair (EV-016). The [2009 Pro-Q announcement](https://www.fabfilter.com/press/1258351200/fabfilter-software-instruments-announce-pro-q) names Bell, shelf, and cut forms (EV-017), while Yamaha's [2018 parameter manual](https://usa.yamaha.com/files/download/other_assets/1/812531/synthesizer_en_pm_c0.pdf) illustrates LPF, HPF, BPF, and band-eliminate responses with axes (EV-018). [Korg's official legacy-manual download](https://www.korg.com/us/support/download/manual/0/842/4469/) is retained as a reproducible primary-source acquisition lead, pending page-level extraction (EV-015).

The evidence supports long-lived response semantics. It does not establish an unbroken, stable, axis-less compact-glyph lineage: the historical sources variously use labels, controls, or explanatory graphs. The 1980s and 1990s remain under-sampled.

## Limitations and next evidence

Add at least two 1980s and two 1990s primary manuals or UI drawings, then compare one carefully documented rendering specimen per decade. Do not infer a visual lineage from semantic continuity alone.
