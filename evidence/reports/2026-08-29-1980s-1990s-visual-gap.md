# 1980s and 1990s Historical Visual-Gap Report

## Scope

This pass tests the under-sampled 1980s and 1990s with four manufacturer owner manuals. It separates period filter terminology from visual rendering evidence and does not infer a compact-glyph lineage from semantic continuity.

## Page-level findings

- Korg's 1986 [DSS-1 Owner's Manual](https://cdn.korg.com/us/support/download/files/6c1d8a2715773887c2d200aea4dbc289.pdf), PDF and printed page 30, places a labeled VCF block in the signal path after two oscillators and before the VCA. It provides period filter terminology and architecture, but no filter-response curve or standalone selector glyph on that page (EV-033).
- Roland's 1987 [D-50 Owner's Manual](https://archive.org/details/synthmanual-roland-d-50-owners-manual), PDF page 69 and printed page 37, describes TVF cutoff frequency and shows labeled, axis-bearing low-pass response sketches. The sketches are explanatory graphs, not axis-less standalone glyphs (EV-034).
- Roland's 1991 [JV-80 Owner's Manual](https://archive.org/details/synthmanual-roland-jv-80-owners-manual), PDF and printed page 45, names LPF and HPF and places two small, labeled, axis-bearing response diagrams beside the filter-type display. This is direct period evidence for a paired response-curve convention, but the labels, axes, and product-screen context remain integral (EV-035).
- Roland's 1996 [XP-60/XP-80 Owner's Manual](https://static.roland.com/assets/media/pdf/XP-60_80_OM.pdf), PDF page 56 and printed page 54, defines OFF, LPF, BPF, HPF, and PKG and shows LPF as an LCD parameter value. It supplies a broader named family but no standalone response-curve selector glyph on that page (EV-036).

The result closes the sampling target of two page-level manufacturer manuals per decade. It strengthens the historical case for stable filter semantics and for axis-bearing response sketches as an explanatory convention. It does not establish an unbroken or standardized axis-less compact-glyph lineage.

## Rejected and negative evidence

The official Korg M1 scan was not counted as an 1980s specimen. Although the product launched in 1988, the reviewed manual's colophon is dated 1991; treating it as 1980s evidence would conflate product introduction with the date of the actual source edition.

The DSS-1 and XP-60/XP-80 pages are material negative findings: both communicate filter meaning through abbreviations, prose, block diagrams, or LCD labels without standalone response glyphs. The D-50 and JV-80 pages use axes and labels. None of the four sources demonstrates portable plain-text use.

## Reuse boundary

All four manuals are linked only as evidence. No manufacturer artwork, screenshots, scans, contours, or typography are copied, traced, or imported. The diagrams may inform semantic and historical analysis but must not be used as source artwork for registry glyphs.

## Follow-up

A later rendering study may compare original neutral specimens against the observed semantic features. Any such study should cite these pages, draw new geometry from the registry's artwork criteria, and preserve the distinction between an explanatory graph and a standalone glyph.
